I am a third-year PhD student in mathematics at Imperial College London, supervised by Prof. Colin Cotter. I hold a President’s PhD Scholarship and am aligned with the EPSRC Centre for Doctoral Training in Mathematics for our Future Climate.

My research is on fast, robust solvers for implicit time stepping in ocean and atmosphere models. Implicit methods need the solution of a large, ill-conditioned linear system at every time step, and on thin domains, where the meshes are strongly anisotropic, standard point-smoother preconditioners lose their effectiveness. I design patch-based multigrid preconditioners for the incompressible Boussinesq equations whose iteration counts stay robust to the aspect ratio and the mesh spacing, and implement them as parallel code in Firedrake and PETSc. I am now extending this work to the Lagrangian-averaged Boussinesq (alpha) model and to three dimensions.

Before the PhD I took an MSci in Mathematics at Imperial with First Class Honours. Along the way I worked on parallel-in-time preconditioning for optimal control of the wave equation, matrix-free solvers for thermoacoustic stability analysis at the Technical University of Munich, and bounded discontinuous Galerkin transport for atmospheric flows.

Research interests: finite element methods, numerical linear algebra and preconditioning, geophysical fluid dynamics, high-performance computing, geometric mechanics and geotechnical engineering.

Outside research I enjoy hiking, tennis, cycling, astrophotography and calligraphy.
