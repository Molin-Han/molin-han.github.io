Professional Summary

Third-year PhD student in computational mathematics at Imperial College London, developing finite element discretisations and robust iterative solvers for geophysical fluid dynamics, implemented as parallel code in Firedrake and PETSc. Research interests include finite element methods, fluid dynamics, numerical linear algebra, high-performance computing, geometric mechanics and geotechnical engineering.

Experience

  • 2024 - Present

    London, UK

    PhD research: robust finite element solvers for ocean models
    Department of Mathematics, Imperial College London
    • Designing preconditioners for implicit time stepping in ocean and atmosphere models, where every time step requires the solution of a large, ill-conditioned linear system and anisotropic meshes on thin domains defeat standard point smoothers.
    • Developed a patch-wise preconditioner for mixed Poisson-type systems: a shift eliminates the pressure, leaving a grad-div Helmholtz-type problem solved by multigrid with additive Schwarz line smoothers.
    • Extended the method to the fully nonlinear incompressible Boussinesq equations on a vertical slice, using a shifted auxiliary operator and multigrid with vertical vertex-patch line smoothers; the shift is set by a bound derived from the system linearised about a state of rest.
    • Iteration counts are robust to aspect ratio and mesh spacing, with shorter solve times than standard methods; validated on the Skamarock–Klemp gravity-wave test case in parallel MPI runs on an HPC cluster.
    • Implemented in Firedrake, PETSc and Irksome; contributed to Firedrake’s additive Schwarz patch preconditioners (PR #5211). Now extending the solver to the Lagrangian-averaged Boussinesq (alpha) model and to three dimensions.
